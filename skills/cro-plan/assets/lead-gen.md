# Lead Generation CRO Template

Industry template for lead generation businesses. Applies to B2B companies,
agencies, consultancies, financial services, insurance, real estate, education,
and any business where the primary online conversion is capturing contact
information for sales follow-up.

## Common CRO Patterns for Lead Gen

1. **Progressive profiling** -- Ask for minimal information on first
   interaction (name + email). On subsequent interactions, request additional
   fields (company, role, phone). Reduces initial friction while building a
   complete lead profile over time.

2. **Multi-step forms** -- Break long forms into 2-3 steps with a progress
   bar. First step captures email (the minimum viable lead), subsequent steps
   collect qualifying information. 15-25% higher completion than single-page
   long forms.

3. **Content upgrades** -- Blog post-specific lead magnets. A post about
   "Email Marketing Best Practices" offers a downloadable "Email Marketing
   Checklist." Converts 5-15% of blog readers vs 1-3% for generic sidebar
   CTAs.

4. **Webinar registrations** -- Live webinars convert 20-40% of landing page
   visitors. On-demand replays convert 10-20%. Both generate high-quality
   leads because attendees invest time.

5. **Calculator and assessment tools** -- Interactive tools (ROI calculators,
   maturity assessments, cost estimators) generate leads while providing value.
   Results are gated behind email capture. 30-50% completion rates.

6. **Exit-intent lead capture** -- When visitors move to leave, offer a
   relevant lead magnet. Less aggressive than immediate popups. Captures
   3-5% of abandoning visitors.

7. **Social proof near forms** -- Testimonials, client logos, or "Join 5,000+
   marketers" messaging immediately adjacent to the form. Reduces form
   abandonment by 10-20%.

8. **Thank you page optimization** -- After form submission, do not waste the
   thank you page. Offer a next step: book a call, join a community, share
   with colleagues, download additional resources.

## Quick Win Checklist

- [ ] Reduce primary form to 3-4 fields maximum (name, email, company, phone)
- [ ] Add social proof (logos or testimonial) directly adjacent to the form
- [ ] Change form submit button from "Submit" to benefit-oriented text ("Get My Free Guide")
- [ ] Add privacy reassurance ("We respect your privacy. Unsubscribe anytime.")
- [ ] Add a content upgrade CTA to the top 5 blog posts by traffic
- [ ] Implement exit-intent popup with lead magnet on high-traffic pages
- [ ] Add inline CTAs within blog posts (not just sidebar or bottom)
- [ ] Optimize thank you page with a secondary conversion (book a call, share)
- [ ] Add form to homepage above the fold (not just on the contact page)
- [ ] Display lead magnet preview image (ebook cover, report sample) near download form

## Recommended Test Ideas

| Test | Hypothesis | ICE Estimate |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| Form: single-step vs multi-step | Multi-step form will increase completion because the first step feels easy (just email) | 7.3 |
| Landing page: long-form vs short-form | Short-form landing page will increase conversions for warm traffic because less reading = less friction | 6.0 |
| CTA: "Download Free Guide" vs "Get Instant Access" | "Get Instant Access" will increase clicks because "instant" triggers urgency | 6.7 |
| Form placement: sidebar vs inline vs popup | Inline form within content will outperform sidebar because it appears in the natural reading flow | 6.3 |
| Lead magnet: ebook vs checklist vs template | Checklist format will generate more leads because it promises immediate, actionable value | 5.7 |
| Form: with phone field vs without phone | Removing phone field will increase form completions because it is the highest-friction field | 7.7 |
| Thank you page: plain vs next-step CTA | Adding "Book a Call" on the thank you page will generate more SQLs because momentum is highest post-conversion | 7.0 |
| Social proof: logos only vs logos + testimonial | Adding a testimonial quote next to the form will increase submissions because specific stories outperform logos alone | 6.3 |
