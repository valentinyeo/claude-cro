# Psychology Principles for CRO

Persuasion frameworks, cognitive biases, and behavioral models applied to
conversion rate optimization. Use these principles to evaluate existing
persuasion tactics and recommend evidence-based improvements.

---

## Cialdini's 7 Principles of Persuasion

### 1. Reciprocity

People feel obligated to return favors. Give value before asking for commitment.

**Web Applications:**
- Free tools, calculators, or assessments before asking for signup
- Valuable content (guides, templates, checklists) gated behind email
- Free trials with full feature access
- Generous free tier that creates dependency
- Free shipping thresholds that encourage larger orders

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the site offer something valuable before asking for the conversion?
- Is the free value genuinely useful (not just a teaser)?
- Is the reciprocity trigger positioned before the CTA?

---

### 2. Commitment and Consistency

People want to act consistently with their past choices. Start with small asks.

**Web Applications:**
- Micro-commitments: quiz, configurator, or assessment before signup
- Progressive profiling (ask for email first, details later)
- "Yes ladder" — series of small agreements leading to conversion
- Saved preferences, wishlists, and customizations
- Free trial that builds user investment before paywall
- Multi-step forms that leverage completion bias

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the site use progressive disclosure or stepped funnels?
- Are micro-commitments present before the main conversion ask?
- Is the funnel structured to build momentum?

---

### 3. Social Proof

People look to others' behavior to determine correct action, especially under
uncertainty.

**Web Applications:**
- Customer testimonials with names, photos, and specifics
- Review counts and star ratings prominently displayed
- "X customers served" or "Y users trust us" counters
- Client logos (especially recognizable brands)
- Case studies with measurable results
- Real-time activity notifications ("John from NYC just purchased")
- User-generated content (photos, videos)
- Media mentions and press logos ("As seen in...")
- Community size indicators

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Is social proof present above the fold?
- Is it specific (numbers, names, results) rather than vague?
- Does it match the target audience (peer proof vs. aspirational)?
- Is there enough volume to be convincing (5 reviews vs. 500)?
- Is it recent and verifiable?

---

### 4. Authority

People defer to experts and credible sources.

**Web Applications:**
- Expert endorsements, certifications, and awards
- Industry credentials and compliance badges (SOC 2, GDPR, ISO)
- Author bios with qualifications on content pages
- Partnership logos (Google Partner, Shopify Plus, etc.)
- Media features and press coverage
- Professional photography and design quality (signals investment)
- Data-driven claims with cited sources
- Patent or proprietary technology mentions

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the site establish credibility early?
- Are authority signals relevant to the audience's concerns?
- Is expertise demonstrated (not just claimed)?

---

### 5. Liking

People are more easily persuaded by those they like. Similarity, attractiveness,
and familiarity drive liking.

**Web Applications:**
- Founder story and team photos (humanize the brand)
- Brand voice that matches target audience tone
- Relatable customer stories and use cases
- Behind-the-scenes content
- Shared values and mission statements
- Community involvement and social responsibility
- Personalization ("recommended for you")
- Conversational UI and chatbot personality

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the brand feel human and relatable?
- Does the voice/tone match the target audience?
- Are there elements that create emotional connection?

---

### 6. Scarcity

People value things more when they are rare or diminishing in availability.

**Web Applications:**
- Limited-time offers with countdown timers
- Low stock indicators ("Only 3 left")
- Limited spots or seats ("5 seats remaining in this cohort")
- Exclusive or members-only access
- Seasonal or one-time offers
- Early-bird pricing with deadline
- "Selling fast" or "popular item" badges
- Waitlist for high-demand features

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Is scarcity genuine (not manufactured)?
- Is the scarcity signal specific (numbers, dates) rather than vague?
- Is it used sparingly (overuse triggers skepticism)?
- Does it create urgency without feeling manipulative?

**Dark Pattern Warning:** Fake scarcity (countdown timers that reset, fabricated
stock levels) destroys trust when discovered. Always flag fake scarcity as a
critical issue.

---

### 7. Unity

People are influenced by shared identity — group membership, shared values,
and belonging.

**Web Applications:**
- Community membership and forums
- "Join X professionals who..." framing
- Shared identity language ("Built for developers", "For busy moms")
- Co-creation and user input on product direction
- Exclusive groups or insider programs
- Values-driven messaging ("We believe...")
- Geographic or cultural affinity
- Industry-specific language and jargon (shows insider knowledge)

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the site create a sense of belonging?
- Is the target audience explicitly named and spoken to?
- Is there community or co-creation present?

## Key Cognitive Biases for CRO

### Anchoring Effect
People rely heavily on the first piece of information offered (the "anchor").

**CRO Application:**
- Show the highest-priced plan first on pricing pages
- Display original price before discounted price (strikethrough pricing)
- Lead with the most impressive metric or result
- "Compare at $X" reference pricing
- Start negotiations from a high point

**Test Idea:** Reorder pricing tiers to lead with the premium plan.

---

### Loss Aversion
Losses feel roughly 2x more painful than equivalent gains feel good.

**CRO Application:**
- "Don't miss out" and "Don't lose your progress" messaging
- Free trial expiration reminders emphasizing what they'll lose
- Cart abandonment emails framing items as "about to sell out"
- Savings calculators showing cost of inaction
- "You're leaving money on the table" framing

**Test Idea:** Reframe CTA from "Start saving" to "Stop losing $X/month."

---

### Framing Effect
The same information presented differently leads to different decisions.

**CRO Application:**
- "$1/day" vs "$365/year" (daily framing feels cheaper)
- "90% fat-free" vs "10% fat" (positive framing)
- "Save 20%" vs "$50 off" (choose the one that sounds larger)
- "Join 10,000+ users" vs "We have customers" (specific vs. vague)
- Monthly vs. annual pricing emphasis

**Test Idea:** Test "Save $240/year" vs "Save $20/month" on billing toggle.

---

### Bandwagon Effect
People adopt behaviors that others are adopting, especially in uncertain
situations.

**CRO Application:**
- "Most popular" badge on pricing plans
- "Bestseller" and "trending" tags on products
- Growing user count displayed prominently
- Real-time purchase notifications
- "X people are viewing this right now"

**Test Idea:** Add "Most Popular" badge to the middle pricing tier.

---

### Endowment Effect
People overvalue things they feel ownership of, even if that ownership is
temporary or imagined.

**CRO Application:**
- Free trials with full access (users build attachment)
- Freemium models where users invest time and data
- Product configurators and customizers ("your custom design")
- Personalized dashboards and saved preferences
- "Your cart" and "Your wishlist" language (possessive framing)

**Test Idea:** Change "Add to cart" to "Make it yours."

---

### Status Quo Bias
People prefer things to stay the same. Defaults are powerful.

**CRO Application:**
- Pre-selected options (annual billing, recommended plan)
- Opt-out rather than opt-in for newsletters (within legal limits)
- Default shipping method set to standard
- Pre-checked "keep me logged in"
- Auto-renewal as default

**Test Idea:** Pre-select the annual billing option on the pricing page.

---

### Choice Overload (Paradox of Choice)
Too many options lead to decision paralysis and lower satisfaction.

**CRO Application:**
- Limit pricing plans to 3-4 options
- Highlight a recommended/default option
- Use comparison tables to simplify complex choices
- Progressive filtering for large product catalogs
- Curated collections and "editor's picks"
- Reduce form field options where possible

**Test Idea:** Reduce pricing tiers from 5 to 3 with a highlighted "recommended."

---

### IKEA Effect
People value things more when they have contributed to creating them.

**CRO Application:**
- Product customizers and configurators
- Onboarding flows that let users set up their workspace
- Quiz-based recommendations ("Your personalized plan")
- User-generated content opportunities
- Co-creation features

**Test Idea:** Add a short quiz before showing product recommendations.

## Fogg Behavior Model

**B = MAP** — Behavior occurs when Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt converge
at the same moment.

### Motivation
The user's desire to complete the action.

**Increase Motivation By:**
- Communicating clear value proposition
- Showing social proof and success stories
- Creating emotional resonance (pain points, aspirations)
- Reducing perceived risk (guarantees, free trials)
- Demonstrating ROI or savings

**CRO Diagnostic:**
- Is the value proposition clear and compelling?
- Does the page address the user's pain points?
- Are benefits (not just features) communicated?
- Is there a reason to act now (urgency)?

### Ability
How easy it is for the user to complete the action.

**Increase Ability By:**
- Reducing form fields to minimum
- Offering social login and autofill
- Simplifying navigation and page structure
- Removing unnecessary steps in the funnel
- Providing guest checkout
- Using progressive disclosure
- Optimizing page speed (slow = hard)

**CRO Diagnostic:**
- How many steps are required to convert?
- Are there unnecessary friction points?
- Can form fields be reduced or auto-filled?
- Is the process mobile-friendly?

### Prompt
The trigger that tells the user to act now.

**Effective Prompts:**
- Clear, visible, action-oriented CTAs
- Contextual placement (prompt appears when motivation is highest)
- Reminder emails and retargeting
- Exit-intent offers
- Urgency signals (deadline, limited stock)
- Notification prompts at the right moment

**CRO Diagnostic:**
- Is the CTA visible and clear?
- Is the prompt timed to peak motivation?
- Are there enough prompts throughout the page (without being spammy)?
- Do prompts match the user's current stage?

### Applying MAP to CRO

When conversions are low, diagnose which element is weakest:

| Symptom | Likely Weak Element | Fix |
|---------|--------------------|-----|
| High traffic, low engagement | Low Motivation | Improve value prop, social proof |
| High engagement, low conversion | Low Ability | Simplify process, reduce friction |
| Good page, bad conversion | Weak Prompt | Improve CTA visibility, timing, copy |
| Cart abandonment | Low Ability or Motivation | Reduce checkout steps, add trust signals |

## Emotional Triggers

### Fear and Security
- Security badges on checkout pages
- Money-back guarantees and refund policies
- SSL indicators and trust seals
- Privacy assurances near form fields
- Data protection compliance badges (GDPR, CCPA)

### Trust and Reliability
- Uptime guarantees and SLAs
- Customer support availability indicators
- Transparent pricing (no hidden fees)
- Clear terms and conditions
- Company history and longevity

### Urgency
- Limited-time pricing
- Countdown timers (genuine deadlines only)
- "Offer ends [specific date]" messaging
- Cart reservation timers
- Seasonal promotions with clear end dates

### Curiosity
- Partial reveals ("See your results" after quiz)
- Blurred content behind signup walls
- "Preview" features that tease full access
- Case study teasers with hidden metrics
- "Unlock" language for gated content

### Belonging and Community
- User count and community size
- Forum or community links
- User-generated content galleries
- Shared mission and values
- "People like you" personalization

## Application Guidelines

### When to Use Each Principle

| Funnel Stage | Primary Principles |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Awareness** | Social Proof, Authority, Liking |
| **Consideration** | Reciprocity, Commitment/Consistency, Framing |
| **Decision** | Scarcity, Loss Aversion, Anchoring |
| **Action** | Ability (reduce friction), Prompt (clear CTA) |
| **Retention** | Endowment Effect, Unity, Commitment |

### Ethical Boundaries

**Acceptable:**
- Genuine scarcity (real stock levels, real deadlines)
- True social proof (real testimonials, verified reviews)
- Honest framing of genuine benefits
- Defaults that serve the user's interest
- Creating genuine urgency around real offers

**Unacceptable (flag as dark patterns):**
- Fake countdown timers that reset
- Fabricated testimonials or review counts
- Misleading pricing (hidden fees revealed at checkout)
- Confirmshaming ("No thanks, I don't want to save money")
- Forced continuity (hard-to-cancel subscriptions)
- Roach motel patterns (easy signup, impossible cancellation)
- Bait and switch offers
- Disguised ads and misdirection
- Trick questions and double negatives

### Dark Pattern Severity

| Dark Pattern | Severity | Impact |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| Fake scarcity/urgency | Critical | Destroys trust when discovered, potential legal issues |
| Hidden costs at checkout | Critical | 48% cart abandonment cause (Baymard) |
| Misleading CTA text | High | Erodes brand credibility |
| Confirmshaming | High | Creates negative brand association |
| Difficult unsubscribe | High | Legal risk (CAN-SPAM, GDPR) |
| Pre-checked upsells | Medium | Annoys users, may violate regulations |
| Vague cookie consent | Medium | Legal risk, trust erosion |

When evaluating a website, always check for dark patterns and flag them with
appropriate severity. Dark patterns may boost short-term metrics but always
damage long-term conversion through trust erosion and negative word-of-mouth.
